# Heuristics — GOV.UK Content Design Pack

Fast defaults with known exceptions. Try these first; deviate with a stated reason.

## Before writing anything

- **Write the need statement first.** *As a … I need to … so that …*. Cannot complete it without inventing someone? Do not write the page.
- **Type the question into a search box.** Whatever a real person would type is your title and your vocabulary. If nothing sensible can be typed, the need is internal.
- **Check whether the answer already exists.** Default is to improve the existing page, not to add a second one. Two pages answering one question is worse than one mediocre page.
- **Ask what breaks if this does not exist.** No answer → do not publish. This kills more bad content than any editing pass.
- **Look at support tickets before the style guide.** The repeated question is the brief; the exact phrasing is the vocabulary.

## Structure

- **Steps when order matters.** Three or more actions in sequence become numbered steps, always. Never bury a sequence in prose.
- **Table when comparing two or more things on two or more attributes.** If readers would build a table on paper to decide, ship the table.
- **Bullets for parallel options, not for chopped-up prose.** If the fragments do not share a grammatical shape, it is prose with line breaks.
- **Prose is the exception.** Reach for it only for genuine explanation and rationale — never for eligibility, deadlines, prices, sequences or comparisons.
- **Split when a second audience appears.** The moment a page needs "if you are a business, instead…" as a major branch, it is two pages.
- **Headings as the answer skeleton.** Read only the headings: that should already be a usable summary. If they are labels ("Overview", "Background", "More information"), they are carrying nothing.

## Words

- **Front-load in three words.** The first three words of a heading or link decide whether the rest is read. "How to apply" beats "Information about the application process".
- **Answer in the first sentence.** Then qualify. Never build to the answer.
- **Cut the first paragraph by default.** Restore it only if something breaks. Most first paragraphs are throat-clearing.
- **One idea per sentence.** Two ideas joined by "and" or a semicolon are usually two sentences.
- **Prefer the short common word.** Use → not utilise. Help → not facilitate. About → not in relation to. Buy → not purchase. Change → not amend.
- **If a term must be specialist, define it once at first use** and never assume the definition travelled to the next page.
- **Say "you".** Address the reader directly; it forces you to name what they must do.
- **Delete "please", "simply", "just", "note that".** They add words and, in the case of "simply", insult anyone struggling.
- **Kill the metaphor.** Journeys, umbrellas, roadmaps, unlocking, empowering — replace with the literal fact.
- **Read it aloud once.** If you run out of breath or lose the thread, the sentence is too long.

## Numbers, dates and mechanics

- **Numerals over words** for anything a reader might scan for or compare.
- **Spell dates out** so they cannot be misread across locales.
- **Ranges use "to"**, never a dash, so they survive being read aloud.
- **Expand an acronym at first use**, then use it — or, better, avoid needing it.
- **Never use bold for emphasis** in body content; if a sentence needs emphasis to be found, it needs a heading or a list.

## Links and titles

- **Link text is the destination**, front-loaded, and works when read out of context.
- **Never link "here", "this page", "read more", or a raw URL.**
- **Title states the outcome, not the topic**: "Renew a parking permit", not "Parking permit information".
- **Do not repeat the site or section name in the title.** It costs scan-length and adds nothing.

## Lifecycle

- **No owner, no publish.** Assign a person, not a team inbox.
- **Set the review date at publication**, not later. Later never comes.
- **Treat unreviewed content as wrong** until someone confirms otherwise.
- **Retire before rewriting.** Ask whether the page should exist before improving it. Redirect to the nearest correct answer.
- **A repeated support question is a content bug.** Fix the page, then close the ticket theme.
- **Revisit within a month of launch** using real search and drop-off data. First publication is a draft with traffic.

## When to break plain-language defaults

- The specialist term is the legally or clinically binding one, and using a synonym would change meaning → keep it, define it, keep the sentence short around it.
- The audience is entirely specialist *and* research shows the plain term causes ambiguity → keep the specialist term, still shorten sentences and front-load.
- Never break plain-language rules for tone, tradition, brand register, or because the source document was written that way.
