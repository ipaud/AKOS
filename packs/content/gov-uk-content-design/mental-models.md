# Mental Models — GOV.UK Content Design Pack

Named models this pack contributes. Use them as diagnostic lenses on a page, a section, or a whole content estate.

## The user need statement

Every piece of content is justified by a sentence of the form: *as a [person in a situation], I need to [do or know something], so that [outcome]*. If the statement cannot be written without inventing the person or the outcome, the need does not exist and the content should not.

**Diagnostic:** ask for the need statement. If the answer describes the organisation's motive ("so that users understand our new policy"), the need is fake — that is a broadcast, not a need.

## The user's question in the user's words

Users arrive holding a question already phrased. They do not translate it into your terminology first; they type it, ask it, or scan for it as-is. Content that answers a re-phrased version of the question is invisible to the person who has the original.

**Diagnostic:** write the question the page answers as a real person would type it into a search box. Does that wording appear in the title, the first heading, or the first sentence? If not, the page is answering an internal restatement.

## Task, not topic

Topic pages collect everything known about a subject. Task pages get a person from a situation to a resolution. Topics feel comprehensive and reliably fail: the reader must extract their case from a superset. Tasks are narrower, more numerous, and each one actually finishes.

**Diagnostic:** can you name the thing the reader can now do that they could not do before? "Understand our approach to X" is not a task.

## Front-loading (the inverted pyramid)

Attention decays sharply from the first word. The most important thing goes first — first sentence of the page, first sentence of the section, first three words of every heading and link. Context, background, caveats and qualifications go after, in that order. Anything that must be read before the answer is a tax on every reader to serve a minority case.

**Diagnostic:** delete the first paragraph. Did the page get worse, or just shorter? If shorter, it was preamble.

## Scan path, not reading path

Nobody reads a content page linearly. They ricochet: title → headings → bolded or listed fragments → the one paragraph that looks like their case. Whatever exists only inside a paragraph effectively does not exist for most readers.

**Diagnostic:** read only the title, the headings and the list items. Is the answer already there? If the meaning lives in the connective prose, the structure is carrying nothing.

## Cognitive cost budget

Each reader has a budget of attention, spent on vocabulary, sentence length, abstraction, cross-references, and unresolved ambiguity. Every specialist term charged is a term not available for the actual decision. Under stress the budget shrinks toward zero — which is exactly when high-stakes content is read.

**Diagnostic:** count what the sentence asks the reader to hold in memory at once. More than one clause of dependency and the cost is being externalised.

## The hidden actor

Passive constructions delete the person responsible. "Applications will be reviewed" leaves the reader unable to tell whether *they* must do something, *you* will do something, or nothing is happening. In instructional content this is not a style flaw, it is a factual omission.

**Diagnostic:** for every sentence describing an action, name who does it. If the sentence does not say, it is broken regardless of how it reads.

## Content decay

Content is not a build artefact; it is a standing claim that ages. Prices, deadlines, system names, legal thresholds and org names all drift. The failure is silent — the page still renders and still ranks — so decay is only found by scheduled review or by a user acting on wrong information.

**Diagnostic:** for any page, name the owner and the last review date. If either is unknown, treat the page as suspect content, not as an asset.

## Retirement as a design act

The content estate has a carrying cost: every page competes for search, dilutes the correct answer, and must be kept true. Deleting a page that has served its purpose improves the estate. Growth in page count without growth in met needs is decline.

**Diagnostic:** for each page, ask what breaks if it is redirected to its nearest sibling. Often: nothing.

## The assisted-digital reader

A share of readers do not read the content themselves. Someone reads it to them, or interprets it for them: a relative, a support agent, a caseworker, a translator. Content must survive being spoken aloud, quoted in fragments, and paraphrased by someone under time pressure.

**Diagnostic:** read the key paragraph out loud, once, at speed. Could the listener act on it without asking you to repeat it?

## Search demand as the demand signal

What people search for is the cheapest available statement of what they need and how they say it. Internal vocabulary preferences lose to search data by default, because search data is evidence of the reader and the preference is evidence of the author.

**Diagnostic:** compare the page's key terms with actual query wording. Divergence is a finding, not a difference of opinion.
