# Glossary — Laws of UX Pack

- **Hick's Law** — decision time grows with number/ambiguity of choices.
- **Fitts's Law** — target acquisition time grows with distance-to-size ratio.
- **Jakob's Law** — expectations transfer from all other products; convention is free training.
- **Miller's Law** — working memory holds ~7±2 chunks; design for 3–5.
- **Chunking** — grouping raw items into meaningful units to fit memory limits.
- **Tesler's Law** — task complexity is conserved; design chooses who absorbs it.
- **Peak-End Rule** — memory of an experience = its peak + its ending, not its average.
- **Serial Position Effect** — primacy + recency: firsts and lasts are remembered.
- **Von Restorff Effect** — the isolated/different item captures attention and memory.
- **Aesthetic-Usability Effect** — perceived usability rises with visual appeal; masks defects in feedback.
- **Goal-Gradient Effect** — effort/motivation accelerate as the goal visibly nears.
- **Endowed progress** — starting users with honest non-zero progress to engage the gradient.
- **Distinctiveness budget** — the zero-sum attention economy of accents per screen.
- **Complexity dumping** — pushing system-absorbable complexity onto users (anti-Tesler).
- **Law laundering** — citing a law outside its mechanism to decorate a taste argument.
