# Engineering Rules — Laws of UX Pack

Checkable rules; law in parentheses.

- LX1. Decision points on primary flows present ≤5 undifferentiated options; larger sets are grouped with labels, defaulted, or progressively disclosed. (Hick)
- LX2. Every choice UI with a sensible majority case pre-selects it. (Hick, Krug ER22)
- LX3. The most frequent action per screen has the largest interactive area of its group and sits nearest the user's locus (thumb zone on mobile, content on desktop). (Fitts)
- LX4. Destructive actions are never the largest, nearest, or default-focused target. (Fitts inverse, NR19, NG17)
- LX5. Touch targets ≥44×44px effective, ≥8px separation. (Fitts, ER8)
- LX6. Dialog action buttons are adjacent to dialog content; sequential wizard controls in consistent positions across steps. (Fitts)
- LX7. Standard objects (nav, forms, search, cart, auth) follow platform/industry convention; deviations carry a recorded justification. (Jakob, R3/R9)
- LX8. No screen requires recall of information from a previous screen; context is restated. (Miller, NG20–NG21)
- LX9. Displayed identifiers ≥7 chars are chunked (spaces/dashes every 3–4). (Miller)
- LX10. Inferrable inputs (locale, timezone, card type, prefill from auth) are inferred with visible manual override. (Tesler)
- LX11. Any flow ≥3 steps shows a progress indicator; ≥6 steps shows named sub-goals; progress is honest (no fake inflation). (Goal-Gradient)
- LX12. Success/completion screens exist for every key journey: they confirm the result, state where the artifact lives, and offer the next action. (Peak-End, NR12)
- LX13. Waiting states >1s are designed (skeleton/progress/context), not blank. (Peak-End negative-peak, NG1)
- LX14. Exactly one visually isolated primary element per decision surface; accent styling appears on ≤10% of screen elements. (Von Restorff, ER12)
- LX15. Distinctiveness is never conveyed by color alone. (Von Restorff × WCAG 1.4.1)
- LX16. Navigation arrays place highest-priority items in first and last positions. (Serial Position)
- LX17. Usability-test protocols for polished UIs record task success/time/errors, not only satisfaction. (Aesthetic-Usability)
