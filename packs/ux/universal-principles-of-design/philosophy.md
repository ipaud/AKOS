# Philosophy — Universal Principles Pack

## Design principles are cross-domain because humans are constant

The same perceptual and cognitive machinery processes a road sign, a cockpit, and a checkout form. That's why principles formulated in industrial design, architecture, and psychology transfer to software intact: proximity groups, hierarchy orients, forgiveness emboldens, noise obscures — in every medium. This pack keeps the software-relevant core of that canon in one place, so an agent reasoning about an unfamiliar design question can reach for domain-independent ground truth.

## The connective-tissue role

Most AKOS UX packs are *schools* (Krug, Norman, NN/g) or *systems* (HIG, Material). This pack is the layer beneath them: when two schools agree, the agreement is usually one of these principles wearing two vocabularies. Recognizing that structure matters practically — it tells a reviewer that a finding backed by proximity+hierarchy+signal-to-noise isn't three findings but one deep one, and it lets the [knowledge graph](../../../graphs/ux-graph.md) link concepts across packs honestly.

## Principles trade off; judgment allocates

Unlike hard floors (WCAG), these principles routinely conflict: flexibility vs usability, signal density vs progressive disclosure, forgiveness vs confirmation friction. The canon's value isn't answers — it's *named tensions*, so the tradeoff can be argued in shared vocabulary and recorded ([conflict-resolution](../../../core/conflict-resolution.md) tradeoff statements).
