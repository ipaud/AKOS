# Changelog — agent-evals

## [1.0.0] — 2026-07-20

### Added

- First complete version: all 17 schema files populated with operational content.
- Principles AE1–AE18, each with an operational corollary, covering the three-example anecdote as non-evidence, a measurement without a baseline as decoration, the three inseparable properties of a golden dataset (fixed, representative, versioned), an eval as a test suite whose assertion layer tolerates variation and grades judgment, the four eval granularities and why none subsumes the others, the grader as part of the system under test, groundedness as an axis separate from correctness, task completion as the outcome metric with step correctness as diagnosis, cost/latency/tool-call efficiency as first-class dimensions, recovery as measurable only under failure injection, the regression bar as a pre-stated number, flakiness as more corrosive than absence, contamination as measuring memory rather than capability, a baseline change as a decision rather than a side effect, unmeasured dimensions drifting silently, a frozen suite discovering nothing, a case that cannot fail not being a case, and a score meaning nothing without its coverage.
- Engineering rules AEE1–AEE76, grouped into golden datasets, eval granularity, graders, groundedness and hallucination, task completion, cost/latency/efficiency, recovery, regression thresholds and baselines, flakiness, contamination, and harness and reporting.
- The four eval altitudes as a decision table — unit, tool-call, trajectory, end-to-end — with what each catches, what it structurally cannot catch, and its relative cost (`mental-models.md`, `decision-framework.md`).
- The grader ladder — deterministic check → rubric → LLM-as-judge → pairwise — framed as an escalation path rather than a quality ordering, with the rule to climb only as far as the output shape forces and the calibration requirement attached to every non-deterministic rung.
- The completion cliff: why twelve steps at 95% each is a coin flip on the whole task, and why a dashboard of green step metrics is compatible with a system that mostly does not work.
- Red-suite triage as an ordered procedure (flaky → harness → uncontrolled comparison → real regression → the case is wrong), with the order itself doing the work of preventing "the case is out of date" from being reached first.
- The contamination gradient: leakage by training data, by the agent's own accumulated context, and by iteration against the suite, all three producing the same signature of a rising score with unchanged capability, and the held-out set as the primary detector.
- Anti-patterns derived from the pack's own principles: the moving baseline, the vacuous pass, the contaminated benchmark, the self-approving judge, the three-example proof, the bare percentage, the correctness-only suite, the all-green dashboard over a failing task, the happy-path golden set, the retry-to-green suite, the threshold set after the fact, the exact-match trap, the pairwise score that became an absolute, the frozen suite, and the uncontrolled comparison.
- Review checklist and scoring rubric that treat a suite reporting something false about the system it measures — a baseline moved to erase a regression, cases never observed failing, a model grading its own output in its own context, an uncalibrated gating judge, contaminated runs, and real secrets in fixtures — as correctness defects, with a hard cap at 59 for any open CRITICAL, a cap at 39 where changes are accepted on demonstrations with no suite at all, a cap at 79 for a single-altitude or correctness-only suite, and no credit for instance-only fixes.
- Source cited: Anthropic's "Demystifying Evals for AI Agents" (Level 2, published engineering practice) — the pack's only external source, deliberately, with depth beyond it drawn from the in-repo worked example rather than from padding the citation list. The pack is organized around the AKOS 17-file contract rather than the source's structure.
- An honest worked assessment, in `examples.md`, of this repository's own `benchmarks/` harness (21 cases, `manifest.yaml`, `runners/run.py`, `docs/benchmarks/overview.md`). **What it gets right:** a true-positive and a realistic near-miss case for every Level A/B rule; every case verified by deliberately breaking the detector before being trusted; the harness itself debugged as a component, with two recorded construction bugs including the canonical vacuous-pass failure where broken path resolution made every negative case pass for the wrong reason; recall and precision printed with their corpus and an explicit regression-guard caveat on the adjacent line; no `--update-baseline` flag because there is no stored baseline to drift; the one uncovered rule (`PACK_EXPIRED`) stated as a gap in two places rather than implied away; Level C substring matching labeled as weak grading and excluded from the recall/precision arithmetic; synthetic fixtures only; reports gitignored rather than committed stale. **What it does not do, stated without overclaiming:** it evaluates a deterministic rules engine, not an agent — so no trajectory evals, no LLM-as-judge (Level C is substring matching against a deterministic offline mock), no pairwise comparison, no cost/latency/tool-call metrics, no groundedness or hallucination metric, no task completion, recovery, or failure injection, no numeric regression threshold and no stored trend (the same design choice that removes baseline-drift risk also removes the ability to see slow degradation), no held-out set, and a corpus of 21 cases. Its Level A/B/C axis classifies rules by detection method and is orthogonal to this pack's altitude axis; in this pack's terms all 21 cases sit at the unit altitude.
- Injectable prompt fragments: a build-mode constraint block, a twelve-step review lens, a suite audit, a result interrogation that returns EVIDENCE / INCOMPLETE / DEMONSTRATION, a red-suite triage, a grader-selection worksheet, and a tight-budget one-liner.
- Scope boundary recorded against `packs/testing/testing-pyramid` and `packs/testing/tdd` (deterministic-code testing, whose apparatus this pack reuses and whose assertion layer it replaces — stated as AE4 rather than left implicit), `packs/testing/qa-checklists` (human release verification), `core/scoring-model.md` (the twelve-lens review rubric and the 0–100 bands, which this pack composes with rather than restates), `packs/ai-engineering/agent-foundations` (agent architecture), `packs/ai-engineering/context-engineering` (context assembly), and `packs/product/lean-startup` (whether the task is worth doing at all).
- Cross-links recorded to `core/scoring-model.md` (bands, anchors, and verdict linkage behind `scoring-rubric.md`), `core/confidence-model.md` (the confidence-versus-coverage distinction behind AE18), `core/authority-model.md` (the Level 2 assignment), `core/source-policy.md` (this pack's own distillation discipline), and to `benchmarks/README.md`, `benchmarks/manifest.yaml`, `benchmarks/runners/run.py`, and `docs/benchmarks/overview.md` as the in-repo worked example.
