# Mental Models — Continuous Discovery Habits Pack

## Opportunity solution tree

A visual structure: **outcome** (the metric the team owns) at the root → **opportunities** (customer needs, pain points, desires surfaced in research) as branches off the outcome → **solutions** (candidate ways to address an opportunity) as leaves off each opportunity → **experiments/assumption tests** below each solution being seriously considered. The tree makes the *breadth* of exploration visible — a team can see at a glance whether they explored multiple opportunities and multiple solutions, or jumped straight from outcome to one solution.

## Continuous interviewing (weekly touch)

At least one customer conversation, every week, without fail — small enough to sustain indefinitely. Over a year this produces 40-50 independent data points, enough for real patterns to emerge (the same pain story from unrelated customers is a strong signal), versus a single 10-person study that captures one moment's snapshot.

## Story-based interviewing

Ask about a specific *recent, real event* ("walk me through the last time you tried to reconcile your invoices") rather than hypotheticals ("would you use a feature that...") or opinions ("what do you think about..."). Stories reveal actual context, workarounds, and emotional weight; hypotheticals reveal what people *think* they'd do, which is a documented poor predictor of actual behavior.

## Assumption mapping

For a candidate solution, list every assumption it depends on (desirability, usability, feasibility, viability, ethical), then plot them on two axes: how important (does the solution fail if this is wrong?) and how evidenced (do we already have strong evidence, or none?). The intersection — important AND unevidenced — is where to focus testing effort; assumptions that are important-but-already-evidenced or unimportant don't need a dedicated experiment.

## Interview snapshot / opportunity mapping

After each interview, extract the opportunities (needs/pains) mentioned and add them to a running opportunity map or tree — discovery isn't "conduct 5 interviews, then synthesize"; it's continuously updating the shared tree as each new data point arrives, so the team's understanding compounds week over week rather than resetting each research cycle.
