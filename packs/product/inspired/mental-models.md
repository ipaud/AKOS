# Mental Models — Inspired Pack

## Outcomes over outputs

An output is a thing shipped (a feature, a release). An outcome is a change in customer/business behavior that thing was meant to cause (retention up, task completion up, support tickets down). Teams measured on outputs optimize for shipping volume; teams measured on outcomes optimize for whether anything actually changed. Every roadmap item should trace to an intended outcome, statable before the work starts.

## The four risks as a pre-flight checklist

Value / Usability / Feasibility / Viability — treat unresolved risk on any axis as a reason to keep prototyping, not a reason to "just build it and see." Feasibility risk is the one engineers are best positioned to assess early; value risk is the one most often skipped because it's uncomfortable (it might say no).

## Prototypes are the currency of discovery

A prototype (paper, clickable mockup, feasibility spike, concierge/Wizard-of-Oz test) is built to answer a specific risk question as cheaply and quickly as possible — not to be production code. Confusing prototype fidelity with production readiness is a common trap: a prototype good enough to test with 5 users this week beats a "properly built" version ready in 3 months.

## Product vision vs. product strategy vs. roadmap

**Vision** — the multi-year direction, rarely changes. **Strategy** — the sequence of problems chosen to pursue the vision, given constraints, revisited quarterly-ish. **Roadmap** (in Cagan's critique) — often mistaken for strategy but is really a list of outputs with dates; Inspired argues for replacing feature roadmaps with a small set of prioritized *problems/outcomes* each team owns.
