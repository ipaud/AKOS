# Heuristics — Escaping the Build Trap Pack

- **The strategy-document test:** does the strategy doc name a diagnosed problem and a guiding approach, or is it a prioritized feature list with a cover page? If the latter, it's a roadmap wearing a strategy's title.
- **The cascade check:** pick any team's current work item; can you trace it up through 2-3 levels to company vision via *problems/outcomes* at each hop, or does the chain hit a feature request at some level with no stated why?
- **The "no" audit:** in the last quarter, how many proposed features/requests did the product org explicitly decline, with a stated reason tied to strategy? Zero is a red flag.
- **Executive-dashboard test:** what metrics does leadership actually look at weekly? If it's velocity/story points/features-shipped rather than retention/revenue/satisfaction, incentives are pointed at the build trap regardless of what anyone says in meetings.
- **Roadmap date-commitment check:** does the roadmap promise specific features on specific dates 6-12 months out? That's a commitment discovery hasn't earned yet — prefer Now/Next/Later outcome framing.
- **PM job-description check:** does it emphasize "manage backlog, coordinate stakeholders, track delivery" over "understand customers, diagnose problems, set direction"? The former recreates the trap by design.
