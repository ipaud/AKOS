# Philosophy — Escaping the Build Trap Pack

## The build trap is a value crisis disguised as a productivity win

An organization in the build trap measures itself by output — story points closed, features shipped, roadmap items delivered on time — and by every internal measure looks productive and successful. The trap is that none of these measures say anything about whether customers are better off or the business is healthier. Teams can be maximally "productive" by this definition while the product slowly loses relevance, because nobody is measuring the thing that actually matters: value delivered.

## Strategy is a framework for decisions, not a plan of features

A strategy that lists what will be built, in what order, by when, is not a strategy — it's a project plan wearing strategy's clothes. A real strategy (per Perri, borrowing from Richard Rumelt) diagnoses the actual problem, sets a guiding policy for how to approach it, and defines coherent actions — leaving teams to determine *how* to execute against a clear intent, rather than handing them a pre-decided list of outputs.

## The product kata cascades intent without dictating solutions

A structured way (borrowed from Toyota Kata) to connect company-level vision to team-level work without collapsing into a feature list at any layer: set a strategic direction → understand the current state via data and customer research → set the next target condition (an outcome to reach) → let the team run its own build-measure-learn iterations toward that target. Each layer sets *direction*, not *specification*, for the layer below.

## Product management is a company-wide capability problem, not a job title

The build trap isn't fixed by hiring better product managers into a dysfunctional structure — it's fixed by changing how the whole organization sets goals, measures success, and grants autonomy. Executive reporting on output metrics, annual roadmap-lock-in rituals, and PM-as-project-manager job descriptions all actively re-create the trap regardless of individual PM skill.
