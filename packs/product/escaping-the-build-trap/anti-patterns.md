# Anti-Patterns — Escaping the Build Trap Pack

## Strategy-as-backlog

A "strategy" slide deck that is, in substance, a prioritized feature list with quarters attached — no diagnosed problem, no guiding policy, nothing a team could use to determine what *not* to build. Fix: BE1 — strategy names a problem and an approach.

## Cascade collapse

Company vision is legitimately outcome-oriented, but by the time intent reaches team level it has been translated into a specific feature list by a middle layer "to make it actionable" — teams never see the actual problem, only a spec. Fix: BT3 — every layer passes down intent, not solutions.

## Vanity roadmap commitments

A public roadmap promising 12 named features across the next four quarters, driving sales conversations and partner commitments — locking the organization out of anything discovery might learn along the way. Fix: BE2 — outcome/theme framing, rough horizons.

## Delivery-coordinator PM

A PM whose entire week is spent in status meetings, ticket triage, and stakeholder updates, with zero time for customer research or data analysis — effectively a project manager with a product title. Fix: BT4, heuristic PM job-description check.

## Silent yes-org

A product organization that has said yes to every executive/sales/customer feature request for the last year, with no documented declines. Backlog grows, focus dilutes, strategy (if it existed) has no teeth. Fix: BT5, BE5.

## Output-only exec dashboards

Quarterly business reviews built entirely around velocity, story points completed, and features shipped — with no retention, revenue-per-customer, or satisfaction trend in sight. Fix: BE4.

## The one-time strategy offsite

Strategy set once a year at an offsite, never revisited as data comes in, teams left executing against a now-stale target condition for months. Fix: product kata's iterative regrasp-and-reset cycle, not a single annual snapshot.
