# Principles — Escaping the Build Trap Pack

- **BT1 — Measure value delivered, not output shipped.** Executive and team reporting centers on outcome metrics (retention, revenue, task success, problem resolution), with output metrics as secondary context at most.
- **BT2 — Strategy is intent + guiding policy, never a feature list.** A real strategy document names the diagnosed problem and the approach, and explicitly does not enumerate the solution's features.
- **BT3 — Cascade intent, not specification.** At each organizational layer (company → product line → team), what passes down is a problem/outcome/target condition; the layer below determines its own solution.
- **BT4 — Product managers own problem understanding, not just delivery logistics.** A PM job description centered on "manage the backlog and coordinate delivery" without "understand customers and set direction" recreates the build trap regardless of title.
- **BT5 — Say no deliberately and often.** A healthy product org's strategy function includes actively declining features/requests that don't serve the current strategic intent — the absence of "no" in a roadmap review is itself a smell.
- **BT6 — Roadmaps communicate outcomes and rough sequencing, not committed feature-by-date promises.** Now/Next/Later outcome-based roadmaps preserve room for discovery to change the solution.
- **BT7 — Annual planning locks in direction, not solutions.** Long planning cycles are fine for setting strategic intent; they become harmful when they lock specific features to specific quarters a year in advance.
