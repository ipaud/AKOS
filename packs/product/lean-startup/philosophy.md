# Philosophy — Lean Startup Pack

## A startup is an experiment, not an execution plan

Under genuine uncertainty (a new product, a new market, an unproven business model), a detailed long-range plan is fiction dressed as rigor — nobody actually knows which assumptions are true. The Lean Startup reframes the work: every initiative is a set of untested hypotheses, and the job is to design the fastest, cheapest experiments that convert guesses into validated knowledge, before committing serious resources to a direction that might be wrong.

## Validated learning is the unit of progress

Progress in a startup isn't features shipped or code written — it's *validated learning*: rigorous, empirical proof that a specific hypothesis about customers is true or false. A team that ships ten features and learns nothing about whether customers want them has produced motion, not progress. This reframes "productivity" away from output volume and toward learning velocity.

## Build-Measure-Learn, entered at Learn

The loop is usually drawn Build→Measure→Learn, but the *thinking* starts with the question: what do we need to learn? What's the riskiest assumption? Only then: what's the smallest experiment (Measure) that tests it, and what's the minimum thing we need to Build to run that experiment. Entering the loop at "Build" — building first and figuring out what to measure afterward — inverts the discipline and usually produces an MVP that doesn't actually test the risky assumption.

## Vanity metrics feel good and mislead

Metrics that always go up and to the right (total signups, page views, cumulative revenue) create a comforting illusion of progress regardless of whether the business is actually working. Actionable metrics (metrics tied to a specific, repeatable action a team can take and see a causal effect on — cohort-based conversion rates, activation rate per cohort) are harder to look at but tell the truth.
