# Examples — Lean Startup Pack

## Leap-of-faith assumption stated

```
Initiative: team collaboration workspace add-on
Value hypothesis: teams of 5+ will pay $10/seat/month for shared workspaces
Growth hypothesis: existing single-user customers will invite teammates
  organically once they see the feature
Riskiest: value hypothesis (we have zero evidence teams want this)
```

## MVP sized to the hypothesis

Instead of building the full workspace feature (weeks of work): a "Create a team" button that, when clicked, shows "Coming soon — join the waitlist" and captures email + team size. Measures value hypothesis (real interest) in days, not weeks.

## Concierge MVP

Testing "will small businesses pay for done-for-them bookkeeping" — before building any software, the founder manually does bookkeeping for 10 paying customers using spreadsheets. Validates the value hypothesis and pricing before writing a line of product code.

## Vanity vs. actionable metric

Vanity: "Total signups: 50,000" (always goes up, tells you nothing about whether the product works).
Actionable: "Of March's signup cohort, 22% were still active at day 30" compared against "of April's cohort (after onboarding change), 31% were active at day 30" — a specific team action (onboarding change) causally linked to a metric move.

## Pivot documented properly

```
Invalidated assumption: SMB customers will self-serve onboard without sales help
Evidence: 3 experiments (simplified signup, in-app tutorial, email drip) each
  failed to move activation above 8%
Pivot: customer-segment pivot — target mid-market with assisted onboarding
  (sales-led), new hypothesis: mid-market will pay for + adopt with a guided
  setup call
```
