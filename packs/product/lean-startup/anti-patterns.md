# Anti-Patterns — Lean Startup Pack

## MVP as "cheap full product"

Calling a stripped-down but still multi-week, multi-feature build an "MVP" because it's smaller than the original vision — without it actually being scoped to test one specific hypothesis quickly. Fix: LE2 — MVP scope justified against the specific hypothesis, not just "smaller."

## Vanity metric addiction

Dashboards prominently featuring cumulative signups/downloads/pageviews that always trend up, used in board decks as evidence of success, while cohort retention quietly declines underneath. Fix: LE3 — actionable metrics are the decision criterion.

## Build-first, ask-questions-later

Shipping a fully-built feature, then trying to figure out afterward how to measure whether it worked — often finding no baseline exists and no clean way to attribute change to the feature. Fix: LS2, LE4 — learning goal and baseline defined before build.

## The eternal pivot-avoidance

An initiative with three failed experiments in a row, no scheduled decision point, quietly continuing into a fourth and fifth because nobody wants to have the "is this working?" conversation. Fix: LE5 — checkpoint set in advance, not avoided.

## Panic pivot

Abandoning a whole product direction after one disappointing metric, without identifying which specific assumption failed or what new hypothesis the change tests — indistinguishable from just changing strategy on a whim. Fix: LS7, LE6 — name the invalidated assumption and the new hypothesis explicitly.

## Innovation accounting theater

Reporting "progress" via activity metrics (features shipped, experiments run) rather than whether the baseline metric actually moved — mistaking loop *iterations* for loop *results*. Fix: LS5 — real baseline, real movement, is the measure of progress, not activity volume.
