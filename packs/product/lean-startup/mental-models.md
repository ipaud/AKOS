# Mental Models — Lean Startup Pack

## Build-Measure-Learn loop

The core cycle, entered by identifying the riskiest unproven hypothesis: **Learn** (what do we need to know?) → **Measure** (what data would confirm/refute it?) → **Build** (the minimum thing needed to gather that data) → back to **Learn**. Speed through the loop matters more than polish at any single stage — a startup's runway is measured in loop iterations available, not calendar time alone.

## Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

Not "a small version of the product" — the smallest thing that lets you run one meaningful Build-Measure-Learn cycle to test the riskiest hypothesis. An MVP can be a landing page, a concierge service, a Wizard-of-Oz prototype, or a single-feature product; the test for "viable" is whether it generates validated learning, not whether it's a coherent shippable product.

## Leap-of-faith assumptions

Every business plan rests on a small number of assumptions that, if false, sink the whole plan — usually a **value hypothesis** (customers will use/pay for this) and a **growth hypothesis** (how the product will acquire customers at scale). Identify these explicitly and test them first; testing a safe, non-critical assumption first just delays finding out about the dangerous one.

## Innovation accounting

A way of measuring progress in a pre-revenue or pre-scale business that doesn't rely on traditional accounting (which assumes a working, understood business model). Three steps: establish the baseline with real data (not projections), tune the engine (a series of experiments trying to move the baseline metric), and decide to pivot or persevere based on whether tuning is working.

## Pivot vs. persevere

A **pivot** is a structured course correction to test a new fundamental hypothesis about the product, strategy, or growth engine — informed by validated learning, not panic. Persevering means the current direction's metrics are improving in response to changes, so the team continues optimizing rather than starting over. The decision point is scheduled (not avoided indefinitely) — teams that never explicitly ask "pivot or persevere?" tend to drift on a failing hypothesis far too long.
